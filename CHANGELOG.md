### Phase 17
- Close the position when stopping (ADR 29, CLOSED_LOOP.md). `hft_live --exchange-fd N --close-position [--close-collar-bps 30]` (and `supervise.py --loop --close-position`): when the session stops for good (shutdown or kill, including a stop that arrives while a routine end is flattening) and the exchange has confirmed that no order is working, the engine sends an immediate-or-cancel order against the position, priced the collar beyond the opposite touch of the local book (on the tick grid, rounded away from the market), and another for what is left after a partial fill, up to 5 in all, each with the full flatten timeout. It does not close on a reconnect, a window exit, a stale feed or an integrity failure. If it cannot (no liquidity, feed older than the stale limit, no two-sided book, refused by risk), the position stays, the segment summary says why (`close_why_not`, `close_risk_code`), `hft_live` prints `POSITION NOT CLOSED`, logs `position_not_closed` and exits 4; the supervisor reports `stop_not_flat`.
- Risk: `RiskEngine::check_close` ignores the kill switch and the loss limit (the states in which closing matters) and keeps the sanity checks and the rate limit; it accepts an order only if it strictly reduces the position with nothing else working. `Gateway::close_order` sends it with type IOC. The hot path is unchanged (perf gate PASS, 797.80 instructions per input).
- Bridge: type 2 is carried out as `time_in_force: IOC` after the price is checked against the exchange's ticker mid (`--ioc-band-bps`, default 100; no ticker, no order) and the notional cap; then the order record is read and reported as accepted, fill (executed average price) and canceled for the rest. A record that is missing or not final is retried through the sync; if the exchange never says, nothing is reported (a wrong "cancelled" would make the engine sell twice) and the engine's timeout fails the close. Other types than post-only and IOC are rejected unsent.
- Fake exchange: immediate-or-cancel orders (fill at the touch, `ioc_fill_fraction`, `ioc_record_status`, websocket match/done events). Supervisor: `--close-position`, `--close-collar-bps`; its exit-4 messages now say a position may remain.
- Tests: 6 close tests in `test_live` plus `check_close` tests in `test_oms` and `test_gw` (both directions; kill and shutdown; not on routine ends or when off; partial fills; attempts used up; stale feed; one-sided book; collar refused by the price band; timeout; grid rounding; the wait for an acknowledged order); `test_bridge.py` 231 checks (45 new); `test_live_tool.py` section 12 (exit codes, event fields, five slow one-lot orders); three drills (26 in all): `loop_close`, `loop_close_no_liquidity`, `loop_close_off_market`. Sanitizers: ASan and UBSan on `test_live`, `test_oms`, `test_gw`; ASan found a dangling reference in my own test helper (a copy now).
- Mutation check: 72 mutants of the new code (`live.hpp`, `risk.hpp`, `gateway.hpp`, `hft_live.cpp`, `order_bridge.py`); 5 did not compile or match and were rewritten. The first run left 16 survivors: grid rounding of a sell, a one-sided book, the attempt deadline, a cancel sent for a working closing order, the closing flag, the gateway's order type and counter, three fields of the event and the drain wait in `hft_live`, a missing type, the ticker mid, the cap at the order's size, socket fills already reported, the record's status. Each got a test. Four were not observable and the code behind them was removed or simplified instead: the redundant sign tests in `check_close` (two mutants), a second `set_reference` from the same book (it made the price band vacuous), and a cap at `max_order_qty` (the strategy's own cap is 40 lots; a larger position would be refused by risk and fail loudly). The rerun caught all of them.
- Not shown: any contact with the real sandbox (IOC status values, whether the record is there at once, fees, slippage beyond the collar). A position is tracked per segment: one left by a routine segment end, or by an earlier process, is neither known nor closed, and only the last segment decides the exit code. Closing has to finish within the stale limit of the last feed message, and the supervisor stops the capture on SIGTERM. Drills are timing-based.

### Phase 16
- No fill lost around a cancel or replace (ADR 28, CLOSED_LOOP.md): after the exchange accepts a DELETE, the loop bridge reads the order (`GET /orders/{id}`) and takes `filled_size` as the authority. Lots it had not reported are sent as a `fill` before the `canceled` or `replaced` report, which then carries only what was left; an order the record shows as filled completely gets `cancel_rejected` / `replace_rejected`. The record is capped at the order's size; a 404 invents nothing; an unreadable record is logged (`cancel_unverified`) and counted. This closes the gap named in Phase 15 and works with or without the websocket. Cost: a cancel is two calls, a replace three.
- Tests: `test_bridge.py` 186 checks (new section: cancel and replace with a fill just before the DELETE, a record larger than the order, a missing or unreadable record, part of a fill from the socket and part from the record). The fake exchange gained `hide_done` and `fail_get_order`. The smoke test's cancel step now also requires `filled_size` on the cancelled order's record (`test_smoke.py`, 33 checks). 10 mutants of the new code, all caught on the first run.
- Not shown: the real exchange's record of a cancelled order (how long it is kept, whether it has `filled_size`). If it is missing, the cancel is reported from the bridge's own tally, as before, and the smoke test says so.

### Phase 7
- Tick-to-trade pipeline (include/hft/t2t/pipeline.hpp): feed packet -> MdHandler (A/B arbitration, decode) -> OrderBook -> MarketMaker -> Gateway (risk, OMS, encode) -> send, as one object with no heap, exceptions, RTTI, virtual calls, locks or syscalls on the path (compiled with -fno-exceptions -fno-rtti in hft_hotpath_check). The strategy now runs on either TapeBook or the production book through book::BookView. When the feed handler loses the book, the pipeline pulls quotes with Gateway::cancel_all (counted as pulled_cancels, not the kill switch) and resumes by itself after a snapshot.
- Correctness oracle for every latency pass (t2t/session.hpp): the Backtester records what the strategy side saw and sent; replaying the inputs through the real Pipeline must produce the same wire bytes (FNV hash over frames). A pass that changes one byte fails (bench exit 2). Pinned backtest digest unchanged by the optimisations.
- Measurement (bench/bench_t2t): tick-to-trade, tick-to-decision, report-to-decision, per-stage split, duplicate-line cost, a cold L1/L2 pass (4 MiB walk before each tick), a capacity sensitivity table, sustained throughput. rdtsc+lfence timer overhead subtracted, platform noise measured, page faults in the hot loop are a hard failure.
- Optimisations, each measured interleaved A/B (median of 5) and checked against the oracle: parked-packet counter instead of scanning 64 pending slots on every packet (t2t p50 240 -> 163 ns), direct level walk for depth plus reciprocal-multiply tick division (core::InvariantDivisor, exact for all 64-bit inputs, proved in the header and tested against hardware division on 3M random pairs) (144 -> 123 ns on a right-sized book), and book capacity treated as a latency parameter (id table sized 3x peak resting orders). Instructions per input 1814 -> 764, branch mispredicts per input roughly 1/3 of before. A grouped-by-4 id hash was tried and rejected (+4-7% when right-sized).
- Analysis without hardware counters (sandbox has no PMU): sampling profiler with frame-pointer unwinding and addr2line inlining (scripts/flamegraph.py draws a self-contained SVG and a self-time table), and callgrind cache and branch simulation. Cache and branch figures are SIMULATED and labelled so.
- Regression gate in CI (scripts/perf_gate.py): callgrind instruction count per input on the replay only, compared with bench/baselines/t2t_instr.json at +2%. Instruction counts are deterministic (rerun +0.00%), so the gate does not flake on a shared runner. Proven to fail on the old code (+120.7%). Baseline keyed on compiler major version.
- Hardening tests (tests/test_t2t.cpp): replay equals backtest on 3 latency configs, probes do not change the wire bytes, zero allocations, every packet duplicated on line B, a gap closed by the other line, feed loss pulls quotes and recovers (cancels only, idempotent, snapshot resume, no kill), book capacity exceeded fails safe. New invariants and tests in test_md, test_book (depth against an independent oracle, grid edges) and test_core.
- fuzz_pipeline: raw and structured feed events, snapshots, reports and polls with corrupted sequence numbers; oracles are decodable frames, contiguous sequence, no new/replace frames while the feed is untrusted, book, handler and OMS invariants. About 2M clean runs; the mutant that removes the early return in decide was caught inside 120 s. fuzz_md now also checks the handler invariants.
- Fuzzer finding about my own assertion: I asserted that a gap is only open while a packet is parked. The fuzzer disproved it within seconds: a heartbeat ahead of our sequence opens a gap with nothing parked, and the late in-order packet closes it. The drain condition (parked_ != 0 || gap_active_) is therefore required; regression test test_heartbeat_gap_closed_by_late_packet added, and the mutant that dropped the second condition survived until that test existed.
- Mutation check: 18 injected bugs across md, book, divisor, pipeline, gateway, session recorder and strategy; 17 caught at first, the survivor (heartbeat gap closure) now has a test; 18 of 18.
- CI (.github/workflows/ci.yml): gcc-13 and clang-18 with release, asan and ubsan, tsan, a 45 s fuzz smoke on 6 targets, and the perf job (gate plus latency JSON, flame graph and self-time table as artifacts). The workflow was never run on GitHub from here; its commands were run locally.
- Limits: synthetic market; shared 2-vCPU VM (indicative timings; p99.9 and max are dominated by hypervisor stalls); hot-cache numbers are a best case (cold L1/L2 is about 7x slower at p50); NIC, kernel and exchange are outside the measured path; no hardware counters, so cache and branch numbers are simulated; the strategy runs on every packet (an event-relevance filter would cut idle cost but changes timing semantics, deferred); book capacity must be sized per instrument (overflow fails safe).

### Phase 6
- Backtester (include/hft/bt/backtest.hpp): replays a public event tape against the strategy through the real Gateway, OMS and RiskEngine over the real order-entry wire format, to a virtual venue. Four time-ordered sources (tape at the venue, order messages to the venue, tape to the strategy, reports to the strategy) with fixed tie rules; configurable market data, ingress and egress latency plus seeded FIFO jitter. After init the loop allocates nothing (alloc trap proves it); the code compiles under -fno-exceptions -fno-rtti.
- Queue-position fills (bt/virtual_venue.hpp, bt/tape_book.hpp): L3 replay where each tape order carries an arrival number, so orders ahead of or behind a simulated order are known exactly. Trades ahead advance the queue, cancels ahead advance it, a trade behind us fills us, a print at a worse price fills us by at least its size. Passive orders only (anything that would take liquidity is rejected: no impact model). No market impact assumed. queue_penalty_pct pretends a deeper queue as a robustness knob. A built-in oracle compares two independent derivations of the queue (arrival order and tracked quantity); the tests and the fuzzer demand zero disagreements.
- Validated against the real matching engine: 250 random injections of one passive order into the same script of other participants; the simulation never filled earlier or more than the engine, filled in 212 of the engine's 212 cases, at the same moment in 209 and with the same size in 189 (it is pessimistic by construction where a sweep ends exactly on our order with nothing behind it on the tape).
- Synthetic market (bt/market_gen.hpp): a latent fair value with jumps, informed takers, and passive flow that leans toward the latent value, driven through the real matching engine. Imbalance predicts the direction of the next mid move 84% of the time on the default seed; this is measured by a test, not assumed. All integer arithmetic, and random draws are sequenced in separate statements: a unit-test failure under clang exposed unspecified argument evaluation order, which made gcc and clang produce different tapes. Fixed; the full report is now bit-identical across the two compilers.
- Strategy (strat/market_maker.hpp): two-sided PostOnly market maker; fair value = mid + alpha * top-of-book imbalance - skew * position, in 1/16 tick integers; quotes clamped to never take and never self-cross; replace only past a hysteresis threshold; position cap including working orders; reads its orders back from the OMS.
- Accounting (bt/metrics.hpp): exact integer PnL attribution, spread capture + inventory + fees = mark-to-market value, asserted equal to the last unit on random configs and checked against the OMS cash and position. Adverse selection per fill at 100 us, 1 ms and 10 ms (realized spread and post-fill move, ticks per share). Fill rate per quote (accepted orders plus replaces that lost their place) and per quoted share. Sharpe from streaming PnL bins, annualised with a stated, illustrative assumption.
- Tests (tests/test_bt.cpp): hand-built queue scenarios, reject and replace semantics, tape rebuild equals the engine book digest, metrics worked by hand, tie ordering, a pinned golden digest, deterministic replay (identical report bytes), 12 random configs for the identity and OMS/venue agreement, queue-vs-engine, signal sanity (imbalance hit rate, lean on beats lean off in 4 of 5 markets, latency costs PnL), quote discipline, position cap, loss-limit kill switch in a backtest, zero-allocation proof. Release gate run at 400000 requests.
- Mutation check: 22 injected bugs across venue, tape book, metrics, strategy and event ordering; the first pass left 6 alive (equal-quantity replace, adverse-selection sign, horizon timing, quote clamp, tie order, strategy position cap); each now has a test. 22 of 22 caught.
- fuzz_venue: arbitrary input drives the real engine and a venue beside it; no findings in 450k runs.
- bench/bt_report: the gate report. Headline run, same-run-twice digest, signal ablation, latency sweep, queue depth sensitivity.
- Limits: synthetic market only (no licensed data in the sandbox), so returns are not meaningful, only mechanics and shapes; no market impact; passive orders only; a sweep that ends exactly on our order is invisible on the tape; reports and orders share one latency per direction; the strategy has no timers and acts only on events.

### Phase 5
- OMS (include/hft/oms/oms.hpp): eight-state order machine driven by one table (8 states x 9 events, each cell Apply, Ignore, Anomaly or Refuse). Tracks leaves, cum, per-order exposure reservation, position, open buy/sell exposure, cash (128-bit) and mark-to-market PnL. Fixed-size order pool with a retention ring that evicts the oldest finished order; no allocation after init.
- Risk engine (include/hft/risk/risk.hpp): ordered checks (kill switch, loss limit, parameters, order size, notional, price band, position, open orders, token-bucket rate limit last so refused orders spend no token); latched kill switch that keeps the first cause; loss limit by mark-to-market.
- Wire protocol (include/hft/gw/order_entry.hpp): length-prefixed big-endian frames, sequence number per direction, exact-length decoders with enum range checks, StreamDecoder that survives any byte split.
- Gateway (gw/gateway.hpp): risk, then OMS, then encode, then send. Kill sweeps cancel every working order and re-sweep after each inbound report until nothing is left. Sequence gap, malformed message or broken stream trips the kill switch. ExchangeEdge (gw/exchange_edge.hpp) is the exchange-side adapter onto the Phase 4 simulator.
- Replace exposure: while a replace is in flight the order reserves old leaves plus the new quantity, and the risk check requires headroom for the full new quantity. Found by the end-to-end test: with the earlier max(leaves, new) rule, a fill landing before the replace let position plus open exposure exceed the limit.
- Tests: test_oms (independently written 110-cell transition spec checked against the table, effects, exposure accounting, eviction churn, risk boundaries, rate limit, kill/loss, differential vs a slow reference risk engine over 200 random configs); test_gw (codec golden bytes, round-trip, malformed input, stream decoder under random splits, gateway session faults, and end-to-end runs through the simulator at zero latency, with latency and jitter, with chunked bytes, on a slow network, under tight limits, with manual kill, with loss-limit kill; checks client books against the exchange, an independent fill tally, and exposure after every step; zero-allocation proof).
- libFuzzer targets: fuzz_oms (invariants after every step) found a real bug in the first minute: a Replaced report with leaves <= 0 corrupted the books. Now counted as an anomaly and the old order is kept; regression test added. fuzz_codec (decoder consistency whole vs split, canonical re-encode, hostile bytes into gateway and edge).
- Mutation check: 20 injected bugs across risk, OMS table, exposure, gateway sequencing and codec; 19 caught at first, the survivor (ack quantity mismatch counter) now has a test, 20 of 20.
- bench_risk: Phase 5 gate. Risk check p99 is about 30-40 ns (gate: under 1000 ns) on gcc and clang, on accepting, replace and rejecting paths. Whole gateway submit (risk + OMS + encode) p99 about 30-45 ns. Shared 2-vCPU VM, so indicative only.
- Limits: one outstanding request per order, finished orders are evicted oldest-first (a late report for an evicted id is counted as unknown), cancels bypass risk, no heartbeats or retransmission, the rate limiter is checked last.

### Phase 4
- Matching engine (include/hft/sim/matching_engine.hpp): price-time priority; Limit, Market, IOC, FOK (read-only feasibility check, then all-or-nothing) and PostOnly; five self-trade-prevention modes (none, cancel newest, cancel oldest, cancel both, decrement-and-cancel); cancel; cancel/replace (shrink at the same price keeps queue position, anything else loses it and re-enters as a Limit that may trade). Trades print at the resting order's price. Storage reuses the Phase 2 flat-level design plus an owner field; no allocation after init.
- Two event streams per request: private execution reports (Accepted, Rejected, Fill x2 per trade, Canceled with a reason, Replaced, CancelRejected, ReplaceRejected) and public book events (Add, Exec, Reduce, Delete). The order of events within a request is part of the contract.
- Exchange simulator (exchange_sim.hpp): configurable ingress, egress, market-data and processing latency plus seeded jitter, caller-supplied time, per-link FIFO (jitter delays but never reorders one participant), deterministic tie-breaking, bounded event queue with visible back-pressure.
- ITCH publisher (feed_publisher.hpp): public events become Phase 3 wire messages, so the simulator can drive the real market data handler.
- Tests (tests/test_sim.cpp): 26 conformance scenarios that pin each rule; a slow reference matcher (flat vector, dry-run FOK) with differential runs of engine vs reference over all five STP modes on a tiny (forces BookFull) and a large book, compared per request on every report, public event and the book digest; per-request properties (two equal sides per trade, one Exec per trade, rejects leave no trace, killed FOK leaves the book untouched, PostOnly never trades); engine feed rebuilds an identical book in the Phase 3 handler (flush every 1, 5, 37 ops); simulator latency arithmetic, cross-participant races, FIFO under jitter, refusals, determinism, zero-latency equivalence to the bare engine, full pipeline engine -> latency -> ITCH -> handler -> book, and a zero-allocation proof.
- Mutation check: 15 injected engine bugs; 13 caught, the other 2 are equivalent mutants (cannot change behaviour).
- libFuzzer target fuzz_match runs the engine and the reference in lock step and traps on any difference.
- bench_match: throughput and per-request latency by request kind.
- Rules documented as limits: ids are unique among resting orders only (finished ids can be reused), a replace re-enters as plain Limit even if the original was PostOnly, a book-full remainder is cancelled with reason BookFull after matching, and processing_ns is added latency, not a serialising queue.
