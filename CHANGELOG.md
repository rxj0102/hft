## Unreleased

### Phase 27
- Latency sweep (ADR 37). `hft_lab --latency-sweep us1,us2,...` runs the same variants and markets at each delay (strictly increasing, 0 to 100,000 us, at most 12) and prints a table per delay, mean PnL by delay with `kept`, and each variant's difference from the base by delay (count of delays above zero, worst). `--latency-axis all|md|ingress|egress` picks the hop; the others keep their configured delays. `--json` gains `latency_axis` and `latencies`; `--events` files carry `lat<us>us_`. Not combinable with `--grid` or `--regimes`. New helpers in `lab.hpp`: `LatencyAxis`, `parse_latency_axis`, `with_latency`, `parse_latencies`.
- Example (2026-10-17, 8 markets x 40,000 requests): base PnL +3.44 at 0 us, +3.27 at 20 us, +2.09 at 100 us (kept 61 percent); no-skew variant +6.34, +5.80, +2.19 (its lead over the base fell from +2.90 to +0.10). With the delay on one hop only, market data and order entry cost PnL and report return did not. Simulated fills, one generator.

- Tests: parsing and every error, axis names, which fields each axis changes and keeps, a longer delay changes the run and equals the single run (`tests/test_lab.cpp`); option checks, a table per delay, json, the means, kept and differences recomputed from the json, event names, digest, the three axes give three results, other hops keep their delays, a sweep point equals the plain run, fees apply (`scripts/test_lab.py`).
- Verification: 27 mutants of the new code. First run: 4 survived (the axis usage message, the sweep running a non-default regime, the base compared with itself, fees dropped in the sweep) and 1 did not compile; after new tests all five were killed. `ci_local.sh` all steps passed; perf gate PASS (hot path untouched).
- Caveats: the delays are added to a queue-model venue whose tape does not react to us; `kept` is unstable when the smallest-delay PnL is small; 8 markets give means that differ by a few tenths, so small differences between hops are noise.

### Phase 26
- Market regimes in the lab (ADR 36). `hft_lab --regimes a,b,...` runs the same variants on the same seeds under named regimes (default, volatile, quiet, informed, uninformed, thin, slow; each is a set of existing generator settings) and prints a table per regime plus, for two or more, a summary of each variant's mean PnL difference from the base per regime, the count of regimes where it is above zero, and the worst. `--json` gains `regimes`; `--events` file names carry the regime; not combinable with `--grid`. `lab::evaluate` takes the market configuration to generate from. Output without the flag is byte-identical to before.
- Example (2026-10-17, 8 markets x 40,000 requests): removing the inventory skew gained in 5 of 7 regimes (+0.95 to +4.68) and lost in the informed (-1.60) and thin (-1.82) regimes, holding 37 to 41 lots against 6 to 23. Simulated fills, one generator, settings not calibrated to a real market.

- Tests: the regime table pinned value by value, parsing and its errors, the across-regime summary by hand, `evaluate` with a regime against the single run (`tests/test_lab.cpp`); option checks, one regime equals the plain table, three regimes with headings and a paired comparison each, json, the summary recomputed from the json, event file names, digest repeats and depends on order (`scripts/test_lab.py`).
- Verification: 27 mutants of the new code. First run: 1 survived (the paired comparison printed only for the first regime), 3 did not compile and were rewritten; after a new test all four were killed. `ci_local.sh` all steps passed; perf gate PASS (hot path untouched).
- Caveats: the regimes change settings of one simulator; the values are not calibrated to a real market; the summary is not adjusted for the number of comparisons.

### Phase 25

### Phase 24
- Held-out parameter search (ADR 34). `hft_lab --grid "key=v1,v2;key2=w1,w2"` expands up to 500 combinations, ranks them on the training markets by mean PnL minus `--risk-penalty` times the mean largest position, keeps the best `--top K`, and runs only those and the base on held-out markets (`--test-seed`, default seed + 100000; `--test-markets`; overlapping seed ranges are refused). Output: training and held-out tables, the paired held-out comparison with the base, train gain, held-out gain and fraction retained per chosen point, the average shrinkage, and a warning when a chosen value is at the edge of its grid axis. `--json` gains `selected`, `test_variants`, `max_pos`; `--events` writes held-out logs with a `test_` prefix. Logic moved into `lab.hpp` (`evaluate`, `parse_grid`, `score`, `rank`, `retained`, `edge_axes`); the normal output is unchanged (same run digest as before).
- Example (2026-10-17): grids of 48 and 64 points, best 3 re-run on 8 fresh markets: the chosen points kept 90 to 104 percent of their training gain; all sat at a grid edge (alpha large, half spread small), with positions of 29 to 40 lots against 12 for the base. Simulated fills, one generator.
- Tests: grid expansion and limits, ranking (including ties on a 60-element list), penalty, retention, edge detection, `evaluate` against single runs and the digest chain in `tests/test_lab.cpp`; option checks, selection recomputed from the json, gains and retention recomputed for two penalties, seed ranges, event file names in `scripts/test_lab.py`.
- Verification: 60 mutants of the new code; the first run left 17: 3 equivalent (two checks that a later check also makes, and a 501-point grid that cannot be built from axes of at most 50 values; two of the three were removed from the code), 7 killed by new tests (tie order, default held-out count, held-out seeds, penalty in the base score, the train gain and the average line), 7 did not compile and were rewritten (all 7 killed). Second round of 14: no survivors. Release ctest 19 of 19, `test_lab` and `test_lab.py` under ASan and UBSan, clang syntax check with the project warnings, `ci_local.sh` all steps with no retry. The perf gate was not rerun: no hot-path or backtester code changed in this phase.
- Caveats: held-out markets come from the same generator, so retention measures sampling noise and grid selection, not robustness to a different market; the grid optimum sat at the edge in both examples; no claim about real markets.

### Phase 23
- Strategy lab (ADR 33). New tool `hft_lab` runs named market maker variants (`--variant name:key=value,...`, `--sweep key=v1,v2`) over N generated markets through the queue-aware backtester, every variant on every market, and prints per-variant means with standard errors (PnL, edge, inventory, fills, fill rate, largest position) and paired differences against the base (mean, se, t, wins/losses/ties, exact sign test). `--json` writes every per-market number and run digest; `--events DIR` writes one `hft_live`-format event log per run, which `scripts/pnl_report.py --qty-scale 1` reads: for all 20 runs of the test invocation its PnL, cash-flow PnL, edge, fill count and position equal the backtester's own accounting. Logic is in `include/hft/bt/lab.hpp`; `docs/STRATEGY_LAB.md` says how to read it and what it cannot say.
- Backtester: optional observer hooks `fill(...)` and `mid(...)` (compile-time detected, existing observers unchanged) and `run(max_steps)` returning false when the budget runs out. Found by the lab: `requote_ticks=0` with `min_interval_ns=0` makes the market maker answer every replace ack with another replace, which was a hang; the lab reports it as a failed run (exit 3). The strategy code is unchanged.
- `pnl_report.py`: mid samples with the same millisecond keep their logged order (before, ties were ordered by value, so the final mark could be a stale mid). Three new tie tests.
- Tests: `tests/test_lab.cpp` (parsing, bounds, hand-computed statistics, event writer, determinism, hooks leave the digest unchanged, event log rebuilt into the report's PnL and edge), `scripts/test_lab.py` (option checks, paired output, json, events through `pnl_report`, livelock exit 3), wired into CTest, `ci_local.sh` and the workflow.
- Verification: 133 mutants over three rounds (`lab.hpp`, the backtester hooks and budget, `hft_lab.cpp`, the `pnl_report.py` change). First round: 24 not killed; 14 killed by new boundary and output tests (integer overflow, upper bounds of three parameters, a lower bound, character ranges of variant names, the unit of md, egress and jitter latency, the derived risk limit, the printed paired difference against the json, the smallest possible p), 5 were code that nothing could observe and was simplified away (a length guard, an empty-key check in two places, a qty maximum that cannot exceed the floor, an empty-sweep check), 1 is equivalent (risk floor 41 gives the same limit as 40 for a cap of 40), 4 did not compile and were rewritten. Second round of 33: 3 survivors, all equivalent (an empty-string guard that the next check covers, a colon that cannot occur in a name, an empty sweep key that the parameter check rejects). Third round of 5 on the p formatting added late: all killed. ctest 19 of 19 (release), `test_lab` and `test_bt` under ASan and UBSan, `test_lab.py` against the ASan and UBSan `hft_lab`, perf gate PASS (797.80 instructions per input, identical), `ci_local.sh` all steps with no retry. Example run: with 16 markets, removing the imbalance lean or quoting wider lost about 17 per market on 16 of 16 markets; removing the inventory skew gained 7.5 (11 of 16 markets) and, on fresh seeds, 8.7 (14 of 16), with positions up to the cap of 40 against 12.
- Caveats: fills are simulated and the tape does not react to the orders; one generator makes every market; comparisons of several variants are not adjusted for each other; with n markets the sign test cannot go below 2/2^n; fees are not exposed; event logs have millisecond resolution. Nothing here says the strategy would make money anywhere.

### Phase 17
- Close the position when stopping (ADR 29, CLOSED_LOOP.md). `hft_live --exchange-fd N --close-position [--close-collar-bps 30]` (and `supervise.py --loop --close-position`): when the session stops for good (shutdown or kill, including a stop that arrives while a routine end is flattening) and the exchange has confirmed that no order is working, the engine sends an immediate-or-cancel order against the position, priced the collar beyond the opposite touch of the local book (on the tick grid, rounded away from the market), and another for what is left after a partial fill, up to 5 in all, each with the full flatten timeout. It does not close on a reconnect, a window exit, a stale feed or an integrity failure. If it cannot (no liquidity, feed older than the stale limit, no two-sided book, refused by risk), the position stays, the segment summary says why (`close_why_not`, `close_risk_code`), `hft_live` prints `POSITION NOT CLOSED`, logs `position_not_closed` and exits 4; the supervisor reports `stop_not_flat`.
- Risk: `RiskEngine::check_close` ignores the kill switch and the loss limit (the states in which closing matters) and keeps the sanity checks and the rate limit; it accepts an order only if it strictly reduces the position with nothing else working. `Gateway::close_order` sends it with type IOC. The hot path is unchanged (perf gate PASS, 797.80 instructions per input).
- Bridge: type 2 is carried out as `time_in_force: IOC` after the price is checked against the exchange's ticker mid (`--ioc-band-bps`, default 100; no ticker, no order) and the notional cap; then the order record is read and reported as accepted, fill (executed average price) and canceled for the rest. A record that is missing or not final is retried through the sync; if the exchange never says, nothing is reported (a wrong "cancelled" would make the engine sell twice) and the engine's timeout fails the close. Other types than post-only and IOC are rejected unsent.
- Fake exchange: immediate-or-cancel orders (fill at the touch, `ioc_fill_fraction`, `ioc_record_status`, websocket match/done events). Supervisor: `--close-position`, `--close-collar-bps`; its exit-4 messages now say a position may remain.
- Tests: 6 close tests in `test_live` plus `check_close` tests in `test_oms` and `test_gw` (both directions; kill and shutdown; not on routine ends or when off; partial fills; attempts used up; stale feed; one-sided book; collar refused by the price band; timeout; grid rounding; the wait for an acknowledged order); `test_bridge.py` 231 checks (45 new); `test_live_tool.py` section 12 (exit codes, event fields, five slow one-lot orders); three drills (26 in all): `loop_close`, `loop_close_no_liquidity`, `loop_close_off_market`. Sanitizers: ASan and UBSan on `test_live`, `test_oms`, `test_gw`; ASan found a dangling reference in my own test helper (a copy now).
- Mutation check: 72 mutants of the new code (`live.hpp`, `risk.hpp`, `gateway.hpp`, `hft_live.cpp`, `order_bridge.py`); 5 did not compile or match and were rewritten. The first run left 16 survivors: grid rounding of a sell, a one-sided book, the attempt deadline, a cancel sent for a working closing order, the closing flag, the gateway's order type and counter, three fields of the event and the drain wait in `hft_live`, a missing type, the ticker mid, the cap at the order's size, socket fills already reported, the record's status. Each got a test. Four were not observable and the code behind them was removed or simplified instead: the redundant sign tests in `check_close` (two mutants), a second `set_reference` from the same book (it made the price band vacuous), and a cap at `max_order_qty` (the strategy's own cap is 40 lots; a larger position would be refused by risk and fail loudly). The rerun caught all of them.
- Not shown: any contact with the real sandbox (IOC status values, whether the record is there at once, fees, slippage beyond the collar). A position is tracked per segment: one left by a routine segment end, or by an earlier process, is neither known nor closed, and only the last segment decides the exit code. Closing has to finish within the stale limit of the last feed message, and the supervisor stops the capture on SIGTERM. Drills are timing-based.

### Phase 16
- No fill lost around a cancel or replace (ADR 28, CLOSED_LOOP.md): after the exchange accepts a DELETE, the loop bridge reads the order (`GET /orders/{id}`) and takes `filled_size` as the authority. Lots it had not reported are sent as a `fill` before the `canceled` or `replaced` report, which then carries only what was left; an order the record shows as filled completely gets `cancel_rejected` / `replace_rejected`. The record is capped at the order's size; a 404 invents nothing; an unreadable record is logged (`cancel_unverified`) and counted. This closes the gap named in Phase 15 and works with or without the websocket. Cost: a cancel is two calls, a replace three.
- Tests: `test_bridge.py` 186 checks (new section: cancel and replace with a fill just before the DELETE, a record larger than the order, a missing or unreadable record, part of a fill from the socket and part from the record). The fake exchange gained `hide_done` and `fail_get_order`. ### Phase 15

A refused or dropped socket is retried (1 s to 15 s) and is not fatal. The websocket URL is guarded like the REST one (sandbox, or ws://127.0.0.1 in test mode).
