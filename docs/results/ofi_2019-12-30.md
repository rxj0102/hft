# Order flow imbalance on a real Nasdaq ITCH day: 2019-12-30

Written by `scripts/ofi_study.py` from the top of book logs of [itch_2019-12-30.md](itch_2019-12-30.md). Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov (2014) show that order flow imbalance at the best quotes (OFI: bid size added or bid price raised, minus the same on the ask) explains most of the contemporaneous mid price change, with a slope inversely proportional to depth. They report an average R squared of 65% for 10 second intervals across 50 US stocks in 2010.

Method: 10 second intervals from 09:30 to 16:00, mid change in cents, one regression per 30 minute window as in the paper. The depth slope is from regressing log beta on log average depth across windows; the paper's model predicts -1.

| Stock | Intervals | Mean R2 by window | Median t | Depth slope (t) | Next interval R2 (t) | Queue imbalance R2 (t) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AAPL | 2340 | 72% | 10.3 | -1.88 (-6.8) | 0.01% (0.4) | 0.00% (0.2) |
| MSFT | 2340 | 82% | 23.6 | -0.83 (-7.4) | 0.55% (2.4) | 0.37% (2.9) |
| AMZN | 2340 | 52% | 8.9 | -0.11 (-0.2) | 2.65% (5.1) | 0.17% (2.1) |
| TSLA | 2340 | 50% | 5.9 | -0.80 (-2.2) | 0.32% (1.6) | 0.01% (-0.6) |
| QQQ | 2340 | 82% | 23.4 | -0.91 (-22.5) | 0.07% (1.0) | 0.82% (4.8) |

Contemporaneous OFI explains 50% to 82% of 10 second mid changes, in line with the paper, and the slope is positive in every window. The slope falls with depth close to the predicted rate (log slope near -1) for MSFT, TSLA, QQQ; the others give AAPL -1.9, AMZN -0.1, each from only 13 windows.

As a forecast it is weak. OFI in one interval explains at most 2.6% of the next interval's change (AMZN, t = 5.1), and under 1% for the rest. The queue imbalance at the start of an interval, the quantity the market maker in this repo leans on, explains at most 0.8% (QQQ, t = 4.8). Some of these are statistically clear and all are economically small next to a one cent spread, so the imbalance lean is a reasonable small skew of quotes, not a source of directional edge. That matches the README, which claims no profitability.

## What this does not show

One day and five liquid stocks. Prices are in cents, so for low priced or very liquid names many intervals have no mid change. t statistics use White standard errors within each window; they are descriptive. Hidden orders and trades are not in the top of book log, so the paper's comparison with trade imbalance is not repeated.
